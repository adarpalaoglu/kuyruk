Broken on purpose.

Each experiment breaks a standard AI service in a way that happens in real life, measures the damage, then tests the smallest fix. Every prediction was written down before the first measurement.

Experiment 1 · The freeze

One person's slow job froze the service for everyone.

You'd never notice because

with one user, the code works perfectly. The freeze only appears when many slow requests arrive at once.

What we did

Sent 50 slow requests, and asked "are you alive?" every tenth of a second. Wrote the same step three ways.

The fix

Wait for the AI in a way that lets the service keep working. And make the health check itself never wait in line.

to answer, written the usual way

0.007 s

Experiment 2 · Pulling the plug

A restart in the middle of a job lost all the work that was left.

You'd never notice because

the job still says "running", and the health check still says everything is fine. There's no error. It just never finishes.

What we did

Killed the program halfway through a job, with no warning, like a power cut. 25 times without the fix, 30 times with it, including 5 with the real AI.

The fix

A separate worker with a heartbeat. When it goes quiet for 30 seconds, another worker carries on from the first unfinished text.

Experiment 3 · The package

Every update shipped a 1.3 GB package.

You'd never notice because

it still works. It's just slow to send, slow to start, and every one-line change rebuilds everything.

What we did

Built five versions that each change one thing, 120 builds on two different machines.

The surprise

One line, the starting image, did 98% of the work. The fancy tricks added 2%. Build order is what makes rebuilds fast.

Experiment 4 · The automatic checks

The speed-up everyone recommends did nothing.

You'd never notice because

nobody measures a cache after adding it. It's in every guide, so it must help.

What we did

Ran the checks 20 times on GitHub, half with the cache and half without, on the same code.

The lesson

Downloading took under half a second, so there was nothing to save. Measure before you optimise. And the publishing step, which holds the secret, ran only for real updates: skipped in all 21 test runs.
